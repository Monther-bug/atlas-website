### ⚠ The proposal describes a different business

Both proposals say Atlas *"trades, supplies and distributes heavy equipment and industrial machinery"*. Nothing from Atlas supports this. The logo, the Facebook page and every photo point to **crane hire, heavy transport, handling and oil services**. The proposal's structure still works with one change: "Equipment (up to 20 items)" becomes **"Our Fleet"**. But the wording in the proposal, the hero headline and the Services section should follow what Atlas says about itself.

## 3. Visual identity analysis

**The logo [fact]:** a round emblem containing:
- hand-lettered bold Arabic: أطلس الصحراء / للخدمات النفطية;
- an oil derrick with a red flame;
- a black world map circled by a red orbit arrow;
- a line drawing of a mobile crane truck;
- "For oil services" in bold sans-serif;
- **ATLAS ALSHRAA** in widely spaced capitals, set along the bottom arc.

**Brand colours.** I sampled these from the logo badge in two different photos and got the same result both times:

| Colour | Hex | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Atlas Navy | **#23207A** | White text on it: 13.5 : 1 |
| Atlas Red | **#C9221C** | On white: 5.6 : 1 (passes AA for text). On navy: only 2.4 : 1, so **never put red text on navy** |
| Ink | ≈ #1E1E20 | Map and crane line art |
| White | #FFFFFF | Badge ground |

**Strengths.** The logo is easy to recognise and says exactly what the company does. Navy and red are a strong, flag-like pair. Navy also sits well next to the blue skies in every photo.

**Weaknesses that affect the website:**
1. The illustration is too detailed to read below about 56 px, which is a problem for the header, favicon and the small badge on mobile.
2. There is no horizontal lockup and no wordmark-only version.
3. The English spelling is inconsistent: "ALSHRAA" on the logo, "alsahra" in the email, and the correct transliteration would be "Al-Sahraa".
4. We only have a pasted image. **We need the source file** (AI, EPS, SVG or PDF), or at least the PNG saved into the project.

**Recommendation [proposal]:** keep the logo as it is. Logo design is outside the proposal's scope. Use the badge at sizes where it can be read, and in the header pair it with the name set in type (أطلس الصحراء · Atlas Alshraa). Get Atlas's approval for this lockup before using it.

**A motif from the machines themselves [proposal].** Every counterweight and outrigger in the photos carries red-and-white diagonal hazard chevrons (IMG_6360, 6364, 6374, 6375, 6397, 6398). Together with the logo's red orbit arrow, this gives an authentic graphic language:
- thin red diagonal stripes for dividers, the scroll-progress bar and "active" states;
- fine technical lines that look like a crane load chart (radius against capacity) behind fleet data.

## 7. Target audience

1. **Site and project managers at construction contractors** need a specific capacity on a specific date. They decide fast, usually from a phone, and often use WhatsApp.
2. **Procurement teams at oil & gas operators and service companies** need proof: fleet list, certificates, insurance and safety. They read on desktop and forward links.
3. **Port, coastal and infrastructure contractors** need heavy transport and lifting together.

**Inference:** most visits will be on mobile, in Arabic, sometimes on weak connections. So the site must be fast, readable in Arabic first, and offer WhatsApp as the main button.

## 8. Design opportunities

1. **The tonnage scale is the story [proposal].** "From small to the largest" can become the signature interaction: a capacity scale (≈55 t → 350 t) that you move through by scrolling. As you go, each crane photo is revealed and its capacity number counts up. This shows the range in seconds without inventing claims.
2. **Sky as space for type.** The low-angle shots leave large clean areas of blue for big headlines, so we need no fake gradients.
3. **The machines supply the accent colours.** Yellow (Grove/Faun), white (Terex), teal (Kobelco) and red (QUY) come from the photos. The UI stays navy, white and red, so the photography carries the colour.
4. **A hazard-chevron motif** drawn from the equipment (see section 3).
5. **"Ask about this crane"** on every fleet card opens WhatsApp with the model already filled in.
6. **Arabic as the default experience,** not a mirrored afterthought.

## 9. Missing information and assets to request from Atlas

1. The **logo source file** (vector) and approval for a header lockup.
2. **Clean photo exports** without the watermark, plus the **Facebook video** source.
3. **Fleet sheet:** model, type, max capacity, quantity, owned or partner, for each crane.
4. **Phone and WhatsApp** numbers, **office or yard address**, map pin, working hours.
5. 2–3 lines for each **service**, especially what "oil services" and "handling" cover, and whether operators are included.
6. **Founding year**, regions served, any projects or clients they can name, certifications and insurance.
7. Preferred **English spelling** of the name, and the **domain** (for example atlas-alsahraa.ly).
8. Agreement on the **corrected positioning** (crane hire, not equipment trading).

## 10. Initial creative direction [proposal]

**"The right lift."** A cinematic, editorial, sky-and-steel page.
- **Colour:** deep navy (darkened from the brand navy) for dark cinematic bands, white and concrete-grey for reading bands, and brand red used sparingly for actions and the chevron motif.
- **Typography:** bold, slightly condensed display type for capacity numbers and headlines. A technical mono face for specs ("GMK6350 · 350 t"). Arabic display and body faces chosen to match in weight.
- **Motion:** heavy and deliberate, like a load being lifted. Slow eased reveals, pinned photo scenes and line-masked headlines. Nothing bouncy.
